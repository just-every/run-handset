### RL-1 — Preparatory flow configuration

*Date:* 26 October 2026 · *Ruled by:* ct · *Governs:* HND-006, HND-105

**Approved preparatory boundary.** Only current native owners may author, read, perform native graph tests and explicitly approve preparatory flow configuration. Every operation checks current native authority. Saving, testing, publishing or rolling back this configuration grants no prompt-authoring, number-assignment, carrier, outbound-call or live-routing authority. Native graph coverage is not an audible or carrier-qualified branch test, and stored overrides are not evaluated routing schedules.

Publication approval names the exact immutable tested version, named current owner and current publication revision. A changed definition is a new version requiring its own complete tests and approval; approval never transfers to changed inputs. A newer draft does not silently alter an existing publication. Stale publication and rollback revisions are refused. Rollback explicitly approves the retained tested version against the current publication revision and preserves both definitions and attributable history. Future prompt bindings and number assignments require their exact tested, approved versions; changing a shared prompt cannot silently change a published flow.

HND-027 inherits broader capability- and company, location, queue and number-scoped flow administration and immediate current-authority enforcement before those roles ship. HND-047 and HND-006 retain composed live-publication and rollback audit. This preparatory boundary completes none of the remaining visual, scheduling, prompt, extension, number-assignment, carrier-test, active-call or live-routing obligations.

### RL-11 — Nuisance-call rule precedence

*Date:* 22 October 2026 · *Ruled by:* ct · *Governs:* HND-035

Precedence (ct 22 Oct): an active emergency-callback window decides first. Within a layer the most specific match wins (exact, longer prefix, anonymous), and allow, block, screen order applies only between equally specific rules. Owner block and screen bind everyone and no personal allow loosens them; an owner allow lifts only owner rules, and a person's own block or screen still applies to calls routed to them. Every outbound 911 opens a callback window of at least 60 minutes (owner may lengthen) in which inbound voice to that number, including anonymous callers, bypasses blocking and screening. Designated emergency and government numbers may stand until an owner-chosen review date. A keypad response proves only a pressed digit: no identity, consent or outbound authority.
